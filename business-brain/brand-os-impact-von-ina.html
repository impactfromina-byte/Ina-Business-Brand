The Core Problems

Problem 1

"I've achieved a lot — but I don't know what comes next. And I don't even know what I want anymore."

Problem 2

"I'm so overworked and frustrated that I can't even look left or right. My head isn't free to think about something new."

Problem 3

"I want to become a leader / go freelance / switch departments — but I don't actually know what that means from the inside."

Why They Haven't Fixed It Yet

They've tried career books, workshops, generic coaching, online courses — and found that none of it translates to how companies actually work. The coaches didn't know what really happens behind closed doors. The books were theory. AI-written applications got nowhere. What they were missing is someone who has been inside, who knows how decisions really get made, and who can tell them the truth — fast.

What They Want Instead

Tangible

A concrete next move — job offer, promotion, freelance launch, business founded — with a plan that actually works in the real world.

Emotional

The relief of finally having a clear head. The confidence to make a decision and commit to it. Being back in the driver's seat.

Identity

From "high achiever who somehow got stuck" to "person who takes ownership of their own path — no matter what."

ICP Persona Summary
The Stuck High Performer
Senior Professional · Self-Funding · DACH Market

The ideal client for Impact von Ina is a high-achieving professional — typically mid to senior level — who has spent years delivering results and has quietly stopped knowing what they themselves actually want. They're not failing; they're often performing well. But they feel a creeping sense of being trapped: overworked, under-energised, and unable to imagine a next chapter. They're not whiners looking for someone to blame — they're doers who've temporarily lost direction and want someone to help them find it fast, with real advice that translates to how organisations actually work. They fund this investment themselves, which means they're serious. And they respond to someone who's been inside, who knows the system, and who won't waste their time.

Content Direction
  • Topics that resonate: The truth about how promotions actually happen. What corporate culture really means for your career. How to make a decision when you're too tired to think. The difference between the title you want and the job it actually is. Why your last coaching didn't work.
  • Tone that connects: Direct. Warm. No bullshit. The colleague who's seen it all and will tell you what nobody else will.
  • Proof they need: Specific examples from real companies and real situations — not anonymised frameworks. Real scenarios. Real outcomes.
  • What to avoid: "Empower." "Unlock your potential." "Holistic." "Journey." Any language that sounds like a course landing page from 2019.

One Avatar: Marcus
Marcus
Senior Expert · 42 · 20 Years In · Privately Stuck

Marcus is 42. He's been good at his job for twenty years. He's delivered — consistently, reliably, sometimes brilliantly. From the outside, everything looks fine. He's earning well. He has respect. But privately, something has gone quiet. The ambition that used to pull him forward is still there — he can feel it — but it has nowhere to go. He's overworked. He's stopped looking up. He tells himself he'll think about what's next "when things calm down." They don't calm down.

"I've done everything right. So why does this feel so wrong? Is this it? I'm not the kind of person who just accepts that. But I don't know where to go from here. And I'm too tired to even look properly."
The Villain

External

A career built on other people's expectations, in a system whose rules were never explained, reaching a point where the next step is genuinely unclear.

Internal

The exhaustion of performing without direction. The creeping feeling that time is passing and something is being wasted.

Philosophical

Someone who has spent twenty years building real expertise shouldn't have to navigate their next career decision completely blind.

"The real problem in Marcus's story is a corporate system that rewards performance but teaches nothing about how decisions actually get made — which shows up as being qualified and stuck at the same time, makes him feel like the problem is him when it isn't, and represents the unfair truth that the people who've contributed the most are often the least equipped to navigate their own next chapter."
One Solution: 3-Step Plan
01

Surface

A real conversation — not a questionnaire. Ina digs into what they've actually built, what they've actually loved, and what's actually in the way. Output: clarity on what they want, not what they think they should want.

02

Decode

Insider access: how the decision they want to make actually works inside the system. What "becoming a leader" looks like day-to-day. How a promotion really gets made. Where the real leverage is. The shortcuts that most people never find.

03

Move

The actual plan — the application, the positioning, the approach, the conversation, the timing. Executed now, not eventually. With accountability until the decision is made and landed.

Strategic Do's and Don'ts

DO

  • Lead with the insider credential in every introduction and headline
  • Use specific corporate situations and real examples as proof
  • Name what doesn't work about other solutions — specifically
  • Show the practical outputs: what does a client actually have after?
  • Speak to the person who funds this themselves — frame as investment in agency

DON'T

  • Position as another career coach — insider angle always first
  • Use buzzwords from the coaching world you're differentiating from
  • Try to serve everyone — the strategic sacrifice is real
  • Compete on warmth and empathy alone — that's table stakes
  • Sell time as an interim without strategic intent or exit
