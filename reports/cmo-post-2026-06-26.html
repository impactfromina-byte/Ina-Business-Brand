A VP showed me his AI chat history last week. Forty prompts. Almost all of them written to sound certain in a meeting he hadn't actually made up his mind about yet. He sounded confident. Convincing, even. That's not quite right, he sounded rehearsed. I see this everywhere right now, especially in restructurings and leadership reviews. Someone pastes a problem into a tool, gets a polished answer back, and walks into the room sounding sharper than they actually are. It works. For about ten minutes. Then someone asks the one question the tool never saw coming, and the certainty goes with it. I don't think the problem is leaders using AI. I think it's what they're using it for. To sound prepared, instead of becoming prepared. To sound decisive, instead of actually deciding. To sound like the answer, instead of owning it. That's the difference between performance and competence. After 22 years in these rooms, you can tell which one you're looking at within minutes. The leaders who actually move up don't out-prompt the room. They can defend the same answer with the laptop closed, in the follow-up conversation nobody prepared them for. That's the only test I trust. I practice what I preach. If I can't explain my own reasoning without the tool, I haven't decided anything. I've just borrowed a sentence. Before your next big conversation, ask yourself one thing: would you still believe your own answer if the tool disappeared? That's the bottom line.
